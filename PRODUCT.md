# Product

## Register

product

## Users

FoodStory serves home cooks and food explorers who want to discover recipes, follow local food stories, and save real places around Ho Chi Minh City. Signed-in contributors use the Food Map to keep personal food memories and share useful discoveries; administrators review and maintain submitted content.

## Product Purpose

FoodStory brings recipes, editorial food content, and location-based discovery into one trustworthy experience. Success means a visitor can confidently find something to cook or somewhere real to eat, understand where the information came from, and save or contribute a food memory without the interface getting in the way.

## Brand Personality

Welcoming, curious, and trustworthy. The voice should feel like a knowledgeable local food companion: warm enough to invite exploration, precise enough to earn confidence, and candid whenever evidence is incomplete.

## Anti-references

- Generic admin-dashboard styling on consumer discovery surfaces.
- Stock or illustrative food photography presented as a real restaurant or its actual dish.
- Long, permanently dominant scan/search controls that obscure the map.
- Decorative glass, oversized orange hover effects, or motion that competes with the task.
- AI output presented as a verified place when the evidence is review-only or incomplete.

## Design Principles

- Truth before polish: show verified facts and provenance; label uncertainty instead of inventing detail.
- Let the map lead: keep place discovery spatial and use progressive disclosure for filters and details.
- Preserve the user's flow: familiar controls, stable API behavior, and clear recovery from errors.
- Make contributions safe: review inferred data before it can become a permanent public place.
- Use warmth with restraint: personality should come from food, stories, and helpful copy rather than decoration.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. All core tasks must work with keyboard navigation, visible focus, semantic labels, sufficient contrast, text zoom, and screen readers. Respect reduced-motion preferences, avoid color-only state communication, and keep touch targets usable on mobile.
